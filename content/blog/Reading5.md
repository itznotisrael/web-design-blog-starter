<h2 class = "header-title-alt"> I Know a Place - Everest Pipkin </h2>

<p> 
    The article compares abandoned physical spaces (like unfinished housing developments) with abandoned digital worlds in Roblox—and argues that both serve as meaningful, temporary places of human expression.
</p>

<h2 class = "header-title-alt"> Alive Internet Theory - Spencer Chang </h2>

<p> 
    This piece pushes back against the idea that the internet is “dead” (overrun by bots and AI) and argues that real human connection is still alive—just harder to find.
</p>

<h2 class = "header-title-alt"> The Internet Has Borders - Anna Rose Kerr </h2>

<p> 
    This archived article doesn’t introduce new arguments or ideas but rather just supports the same idea from the article "I Know a Place".
</p>

<h2 class = "header-title-alt"> Reflection </h2>

<p>
    Honestly, these pieces feel a little unsettling—but in a quiet, reflective way. In the article "Alive Internet Theory" when Chang said, "we are all so online, yet being online feels solitary". It hit harder than I expected because it describes how you are both connected and not connecting at the same time. You’re technically “connected” 24/7, but your not physically around people. To me being an introvert that feels like enternal bliss but to others it can be a curse.
</p>
