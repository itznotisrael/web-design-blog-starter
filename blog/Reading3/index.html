Reading III

  • 17 March 2026

The Web's Grain - Frank Chimero

The core idea is the web is not a blank canvas, it’s a material with its own “grain” (natural constraints and tendencies), and a good design works with it, not against it. Designers often try to force the web into unnatural forms (flashy, complex sites), which leads to bad user experiences. The web naturally favors Simplicity, Scrolling layouts, and Flexible, responsive designs.

ChatGPT is a Blurry JPEG of the Web - Ted Chiang

The main point is ChatGPT (and AI like it) works like lossy compression, it captures patterns from the web but loses precision and truth in the process. AI models don’t store exact knowledge—they store statistical patterns. This explains why AI sounds convincing. AI creates the illusion of understanding, not true comprehension.

Heavy Lies the Digital Cloud - Emily Manges

The gist is “cloud” isn’t neutral, it’s a system that captures, shapes, and predicts our lives, often for corporate gain. Our digital lives (photos, texts, thoughts) are stored in corporate systems used for prediction and profit. Algorithms shape identity (e.g., TikTok feeds) and prioritize engagement over truthThe major concern. The main concern is 'The cloud doesn’t just store your life—it actively reshapes and predicts it.'

Reflection

Reading these all three of these readings terrifies me. All three pieces hint at the same fear We think we’re using technology, But increasingly, technology is defining us. 'Heavy Lies the Digital Cloud' explains the cloud decides what memories matter, 'ChatGPT is a Blurry JPEG of the Web' shows AI decides how knowledge is presented, and 'The Web's Grain' shows design trends decide how we interact with the world.
